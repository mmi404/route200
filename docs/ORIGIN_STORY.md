# CUET_404: Why We Named Ourselves After an Error Code

> **CUET_404: turning the world's climate data into a route that reaches you.**

## The Story

We've all felt this: you click, you wait, and the page says 404. Not Found. Not because the content doesn't exist (somewhere on some server it's sitting there, complete and reachable) but because no road was built to get you to it. A 404 isn't the internet telling you something is missing. It's the internet admitting it has no route that resolves to what you asked for.

That is exactly the shape of Bangladesh's climate-risk problem.

NASA and ISRO's NISAR mission is already watching our rivers and hillsides from space, and it already releases that radar data free and fast: normally within a day or two, within hours during an active disaster. Bangladesh's own CEGIS has tracked the Jamuna's erosion every single year since 2004. None of that is hypothetical, and none of it is waiting on more research or a bigger budget. It already works. It already returns 200 OK, at the source.

The failure happens one layer downstream. The WMO's 2024 status report found that under half of the world's least-developed countries have a working multi-hazard early-warning system, even where the underlying science and data pipelines already exist. On the demand side, a UNICEF-Gallup survey from December 2023 found only 37% of Bangladeshi youth could even identify the correct definition of climate change, in one of the most climate-exposed countries on Earth. The science exists. The warnings exist. The institutional monitoring exists. Public awareness doesn't, not where it's needed most, and the gap between what exists and who it reaches is the only thing actually broken.

We named ourselves after that gap: CUET_404.

Our job isn't to generate more data; there's already plenty of that, sitting at 200 OK, unreached. Our job is to build the missing route on the other end. Because no single interface reaches everyone, we're building two of them: an awareness experience that makes the risk visceral for GenZ and the general public, something you watch and feel rather than read about, and a direct SMS/IVR alert for people in erosion and landslide zones who will never open an app, a plain-language warning delivered the way a phone call or text already works for them.

This isn't a guess that a problem like this is solvable. Bangladesh has already proven it once: the national cyclone warning system cut cyclone deaths from roughly 300,000–500,000 in the 1970 Bhola cyclone to zero in the 2023 Cyclone Mocha, purely by building a route from forecast to household that didn't exist before. We're extending that same proof to erosion and landslide risk, where that route still doesn't exist today. The July 2026 monsoon landslides across the Chattogram Hill Tracts, which killed more than fifty people, made that painfully clear all over again.

CUET_404 is the name for the failure we refuse to accept. The fix we're building is the route that turns it into 200 OK.

## In One Breath

The science exists. The warning exists. The only thing missing is the route between them. We're CUET_404, and we're building it.
