## 2. The problem

NASA and ISRO's NISAR mission watches Bangladesh's rivers and hillsides from space. Bangladesh's own CEGIS has tracked the Jamuna's erosion every year since 2004. None of this is hypothetical. The science exists, and it already works.

What doesn't exist is the route from that data to the person standing on the riverbank when it gives way. A UNICEF-Gallup survey from December 2023 found only 37% of Bangladeshi youth could correctly identify the definition of climate change, in one of the most climate-exposed countries on earth.

The July 2026 monsoon landslides across the Chattogram Hill Tracts killed more than fifty people. The data wasn't the surprise. The silence around it was. That gap, between what satellites already see and who gets told, is what Route200 closes.

---

## 3. How it works

One engine, two tracks. Satellites like NISAR and Sentinel-1 scan the same riverbank or hillside on repeat passes, in cloud and in clear sky alike, since radar sees through monsoon weather the way an ordinary camera can't. Comparing passes over time reveals where land is eroding or shifting, before it becomes a headline.

That single stream of change data feeds two front doors. One is a public map, for anyone curious or affected, showing the change visually. The other is a direct line to risk-zone households: automated SMS and Bangla voice calls, triggered the moment a zone crosses a risk threshold, no map or app needed on the receiving end.

---

## 4. The map experience teaser

Open the map and the Jamuna isn't a static blue line. It's a river caught mid-motion, bank lines from different years laid over each other so you can watch, in minutes, erosion that actually unfolded over seasons. In the Chattogram Hill Tracts, slopes are shaded by how much ground has shifted, the kind of movement that preceded the 2026 monsoon landslides.

If your village sits on a Jamuna char, or your family's land is on a Chattogram hillside, this map is the closest thing to watching your own risk from orbit, in your own language.

---

## 5. Get alerts

If you live in a zone we're tracking, you don't need a smartphone, an app, or a data plan to find out when the ground under you starts changing. Give us a phone number and your area, and when our engine flags real risk there, we send a text and place a Bangla voice call, the same way an ordinary phone call already reaches you. No install, no login, no catch.
